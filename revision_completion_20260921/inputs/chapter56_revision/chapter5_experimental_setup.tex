\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experimental_setup}

\subsection{Data and Evaluation Populations}
\label{sec:evaluation_populations}

We evaluate WANDS~\cite{chen2022wands} and the US English portion of the small-version ESCI test split~\cite{reddy2022shopping}. WANDS retains its full catalog of 42,994 products. We use the existing split of 96 development and 384 held-out queries; the 308 held-out queries with at least one Exact product contribute 21,299 query--product pairs. ESCI uses the existing deterministic sample of 500 queries and the union of their judged products, yielding a fixed catalog of 10,076 products. Its 499 queries with at least one E-labeled product contribute 4,434 pairs. Every comparison within a dataset retains the same catalog and eligible query population. These are evaluations with incomplete relevance judgments, not estimates of access to every truly relevant product.

WANDS attributes are the complete pipe-separated entries in its product-features field. ESCI attributes are the nonempty brand, color, and complete bullet-point fields; the bullet-point field is retained as one entry. ESCI therefore has at most three entries per product. Titles, descriptions, and other non-attribute fields retain their text and section placement. Duplicate attribute occurrences are preserved. Data preparation removes duplicate judgments and excludes contradictory WANDS query--product labels.

\subsection{Encoders and Ordering Schedules}
\label{sec:encoders_schedules}

The sensitivity and pure-canonicalization experiments use frozen \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2}, \texttt{bge-base-en-v1.5}, and \texttt{gte-modernbert-base}, abbreviated MiniLM, BGE, and GTE. Their input limits are 256, 512, and 8,192 tokens, respectively; MiniLM uses attention-mask mean pooling, and BGE and GTE use CLS pooling. We retain empty query prefixes and unit-normalized embeddings. Retrieval uses exact FP32 dot-product scores and resolves ties by ascending fixed catalog index. Pinned revisions and execution details accompany the artifacts.

The seven schedules are source order (C0), reverse attribute order (C1), and five product-dependent shuffles (C2s1--C2s5), using seeds 20260911--20260915. Both interventions follow Section~3. Fully-fitting eligibility requires every complete input, including special tokens, to fit the corresponding encoder limit under all seven schedules; only evaluated targets are filtered, while all competitors remain.

The three canonical rules are fixed and all are reported. Aggregation and hybrid comparisons cover BGE and MiniLM on both datasets. Full-record aggregation uses $M=2$ for this common comparison; the existing BGE sweep additionally covers $M=4,7$. Its view seeds, 20260971--20260977, differ from the evaluation-schedule seeds. Set-Mean uses equal weights, BM25 uses $k_1=1.2$ and $b=0.75$, and hybrid retrieval uses RRF with constant 60.

\subsection{Evaluation and Comparison Protocol}
\label{sec:comparison_protocol}

We report query-macro Recall, persistent inclusion, VI, and persistent omission at $K=20,100$, with pair-micro results kept separate. Membership comparisons use the same $H_q$ and distinguish gains, losses, and their net difference. Exact within-query cancellation requires equal positive integer gain and loss counts. Source-order Recall and the raw seven-schedule mean are separate references; target-only inclusion is not labeled catalog-wide Recall.

Order and canonicalization analyses use 10,000 paired query-cluster bootstrap draws with seed 2026091701 and descriptive 95\% percentile intervals. Each draw retains a query's products and compared conditions together. Intervals are uncorrected for multiple comparisons; containing zero does not establish equivalence. The common aggregation table reports point estimates from saved query-level results. Historical strategy selection used WANDS development queries, whereas the three pure canonical rules were fixed before their additional retrieval runs. Earlier outcomes had already been inspected; these analyses are not a newly untouched confirmation set, and no test-set winner is selected. Cost distinguishes offline encoding from stored retrieval vectors and query-time scoring.
