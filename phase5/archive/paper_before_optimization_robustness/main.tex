\title{Same Product, Different Visibility: Representation Robustness in Neural E-Commerce Retrieval}

\begin{document}
\raggedbottom
\begin{abstract}
Neural retrieval increasingly mediates which Web content can be discovered, compared, or used by agentic interfaces. Semantically equivalent information should ideally receive comparable retrieval opportunities, yet structured records are often flattened into ordered text. We study this representation robustness problem in product search, holding facts and relevance fixed while permuting attributes. Across two benchmarks and three general-purpose retrievers, 4.4--13.3\% of highest-relevance pairs cross Top-20 under catalog-wide permutations. Target-only interventions confirm that the same product can change visibility even with all competitors fixed. We then ask whether fact-preserving strategies improve relevant-product recall, comparing fixed ordering, full-record aggregation, set encoding, and lexical/hybrid retrieval under equal unique-candidate budgets. A development-selected ordering rule does not yield consistent recall improvements and loses recall when transferred to another encoder. Set encoding eliminates measured order instability, while its condensed-NDCG changes are small and statistically inconclusive; this does not establish equivalence or preserved recall. Candidate-budget and fixed-text reranking analyses separate useful retrieval from stable omission: a reranker cannot recover content it never receives. These results support evaluating representation robustness jointly with relevant-content recall, transfer, and computational cost.
\end{abstract}
