\section{Conclusion}

Neural systems increasingly mediate which Web content becomes visible, yet structured content must often be flattened into ordered text before retrieval. Using e-commerce as a controlled testbed, we show that semantically irrelevant attribute order can change whether human-relevant products cross practical visibility boundaries. The effect reproduces across benchmarks and retriever families, propagates into first-stage candidate loss that reranking cannot repair, and disappears under a proof-of-concept set representation with a small, statistically inconclusive effectiveness change. Web retrieval should therefore evaluate relevance and robustness to semantically irrelevant representation choices together.
