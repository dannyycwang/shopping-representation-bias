\title{Same Product, Different Visibility: Representation Robustness in Neural E-Commerce Retrieval}

\begin{document}
\raggedbottom
\begin{abstract}
Neural retrieval mediates access to Web content through search, recommendation, retrieval-augmented systems, and emerging agentic interfaces. Semantically equivalent information should ideally receive comparable opportunities to be discovered. Yet structured Web records must often be serialized into ordered token sequences, allowing arbitrary representation choices to affect visibility. We study this problem in e-commerce, where product attributes admit fact-equivalent permutations. Across two benchmarks and three general-purpose retrievers, 4.4--13.3\% of highest-relevance pairs cross Top-20 under catalog-wide permutations. A separate target-only intervention holds every competitor fixed and confirms that changing only the target's serialization can alter its visibility. Reranking reduces instability among surviving candidates but cannot recover items omitted upstream. A simple permutation-invariant set representation eliminates measured order instability, while effectiveness changes are small and statistically inconclusive; this does not prove equivalence. These findings motivate evaluating neural Web retrieval for robustness to semantically irrelevant representation choices alongside relevance.
\end{abstract}
