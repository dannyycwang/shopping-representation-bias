Crucially, to isolate pure attribute-order sensitivity from token trun-
cation artifacts, we enforce a strict fully-fitting criterion: a target
product is evaluated only if its full input (including special tokens)
fits within the encoder’s limit under all seven schedules. Background
catalog competitors remain unfiltered to maintain realistic search
space density.
Finally, we evaluate mitigation strategies and comparative base-
lines using BGE and MiniLM across both datasets. We report per-
formance across all three deterministic canonicalization rules from
Section 3. For multi-view aggregation, we default to 𝑀 = 2 at-
tribute permutations, supplemented by an extended BGE sweep
at 𝑀 ∈ {4, 7}; to prevent data leakage, aggregation view seeds
(20260971–20260977) are strictly isolated from evaluation seeds.
Baselines include Set-Mean (equally weighted attribute embed-
dings), BM25 (𝑘1 = 1.2,𝑏 = 0.75), and a hybrid dense-sparse re-
triever using Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF, 𝑘 = 60).
5.3 Evaluation and Comparison Protocol
We evaluate query-macro Recall, persistent inclusion, tDCG, VI
defined by subsection 3.3, and persistent omission at 𝐾 ∈ {20, 100},
keeping pair-micro results distinct. For relevant items (𝐻𝑞), we track
gains, losses, and net differences in top-𝐾 inclusion, where cancel-
lation occurs when gain and loss counts are equal. Source-order
Recall and the seven-schedule mean serve as separate references;
target-only inclusion is never reported as catalog-wide Recall.
To analyze order and canonicalization effects, we compute 95%
percentile confidence intervals using 10,000 paired query-cluster
bootstrap resamples (seed 2026091701). To preserve paired depen-
dencies, each resample draws a query’s candidate products jointly
across all compared conditions. These intervals are uncorrected for
multiple comparisons; an interval containing zero indicates non-
significance rather than statistical equivalence. Main table point
estimates are aggregated directly from saved query-level records.
Strategy development used WANDS validation queries, but the
three canonical rules were strictly fixed prior to final evaluation
runs. Because preliminary outcomes were inspected during itera-
tion, these analyses serve as a controlled empirical evaluation rather
than a blind confirmation set, with no test-set winner selected. Fi-
nally, efficiency evaluation explicitly separates offline encoding,
vector storage overhead, and online scoring latency.
6 Results
We examine inclusion sensitivity, its relationship to effectiveness,
and the behavior of order-handling controls. Rates are query-macro
percentages on the 308 WANDS and 499 ESCI eligible queries unless
stated otherwise; differences are percentage points (pp). nDCG uses
the 0–1 scale. Comparisons use paired query-cluster intervals.
6.1 Attribute-Order Sensitivity (RQ1)
Attribute order changes relevant-product inclusion in every dataset–
encoder setting. Across the seven catalog-wide schedules, VI@20
ranges from 11.93–19.58% on WANDS and 3.66–6.91% on ESCI. VI
describes crossing within the tested ordering family, rather than
the probability of omission under future orders.
Changing only the target representation is sufficient. With all
competitors fixed at source order, VI@20 remains 10.83–19.45%
on WANDS and 2.42–5.88% on ESCI (Figure 3). The two interven-
tions answer complementary questions; their difference does not
decompose target and competitor effects. Sensitivity also persists
when every tested target input fits completely: fully-fitting VI@20
ranges from 7.16–14.55% and 2.55–5.88%, respectively. All catalog
inputs fit GTE’s token limit. The restricted populations differ across
encoders, so these comparisons do not identify a causal truncation
effect. They establish that truncation is not necessary for inclusion
sensitivity.
Larger ordering families reveal additional crossing. On a fixed
128-query WANDS supplement, expanding from 7 to 32 schedules
raises target-only VI@20 from 20.85% to 28.21% for MiniLM and
11.40% to 17.55% for BGE. Nested VI is nondecreasing by defini-
tion; the empirical result is the additional affected support. Appen-
dix C.3 reports fitting controls, ESCI target-order enumeration, and
execution-related numerical limits. Native score-boundary cases
and the limitations of attribution-based explanations appear in
Appendices C.1 and C.4.
6.2 Aggregate Effectiveness and Product
Membership (RQ2)
Recall measures the balance between newly included and newly
omitted relevant products; their sum measures membership change
(Figure 4). Reversing WANDS attributes with BGE newly includes
2.357% and newly omits 2.263% of relevant support. Their combined
share is 4.621%, although Recall changes by only +0.094 pp (95%
CI [−0.409, 0.563]). Each query’s denominator is |𝐻𝑞 |. Cancellation
also occurs within queries: across the six source-order comparisons,
67–78 of the 308 WANDS queries have equal positive BGE gain and
loss counts. Their Recall is exactly unchanged despite different re-
trieved products. These are ranges across comparisons, not disjoint
groups.
Graded effectiveness is not generally unchanged. Among queries
with changed Top-20 relevant-product membership, median abso-
lute nDCG@20 differences range from 0.0226–0.0399 on WANDS
and 0.0388–0.0482 on ESCI. Query-mean differences are smaller:
−0.01101 to +0.00151 and −0.00393 to +0.00092, respectively. Five
of the 36 uncorrected paired intervals are entirely negative; the
others contain zero, without establishing equivalence. Aggregate
effectiveness therefore complements, but does not fully describe,
product membership. Appendix C.2 reports conditional distribu-
tions, numerical equality, condensed cNDCG and judged coverage.

Effectiveness varies across settings. Set-Mean exceeds raw dense
Recall@100 on WANDS/MiniLM but falls below it in the other three
settings. Raw hybrid improves Recall@100 while reducing VI@100
on WANDS/MiniLM, WANDS/BGE and ESCI/MiniLM, with paired
intervals supporting both directions. ESCI/BGE differences remain
inconclusive. Raw hybrid retains nonzero VI in all four settings.
Canonical–BM25 hybrids combine lexical evidence with order
invariance. All 12 dataset–encoder–rule configurations have pos-
itive uncorrected nDCG@20 intervals versus BM25, and nine do
versus their corresponding canonical dense branch. Against the
raw-hybrid seven-schedule mean, two nDCG@20 contrasts are
positive and ten are inconclusive; all Recall@100 intervals include
zero. This does not establish equivalence or universal preservation:
WANDS/BGE ascending reduces Recall@20 by 0.624 pp (95% CI
[−1.370, −0.084]). The results support competitive simple controls,
rather than a general effectiveness–instability trade-off.
Cost further distinguishes the controls. Canonical and centroid
retrieval retain one dense vector per product; multi-vector retrieval
stores and scores 𝑀 vectors, and hybrid retrieval adds a sparse index
and branch scoring. Appendix C.5 provides view-budget and timing
results. The practical comparison therefore requires effectiveness,
persistent inclusion and omission, identity transitions, and cost
alongside VI.
