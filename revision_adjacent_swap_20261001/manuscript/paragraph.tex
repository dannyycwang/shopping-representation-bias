A controlled local-perturbation experiment shows that a single adjacent exchange of complete attribute entries can change a relevant product's Top-20 inclusion while the query and every competitor remain fixed at C0 and the target fully fits in all tested serializations. Query-macro affected-target shares and mean one-swap flip frequencies, respectively, were WANDS/MiniLM: 6.01\% [3.55, 8.89] and 0.92\% [0.53, 1.36]; WANDS/BGE: 3.50\% [2.36, 4.92] and 0.48\% [0.29, 0.70]; ESCI/MiniLM: 2.22\% [1.20, 3.41] and 1.44\% [0.79, 2.20]; ESCI/BGE: 1.43\% [0.65, 2.34] and 0.95\% [0.41, 1.60]. Brackets give 95\% unadjusted query-cluster bootstrap intervals. These results concern the sampled fully-fitting populations and enumerated adjacent-swap families; they do not estimate prevalence over all relevant products, share the earlier schedule-based VI estimand, or identify an attention mechanism.
